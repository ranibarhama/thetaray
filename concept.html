The logic
Eight principles behind the design

Dashboard as the entry point

You land on decisions, not data. KPI tiles filter the portfolio with one click; classic by default, ✨ Smart on a switch.

Why: the first 10 seconds should answer "what needs me now?"

One product, three lenses

Analyst works cases; Manager gets workload, comments & assignment; ✨ Smart adds a scored queue, signals and an AI briefing.

Why: same data, different jobs — the UX should follow the role.

Mobile = decisions only

The Manager Pulse is one phone screen: what needs sign-off, live pulse tiles, team imbalance, critical events. Approve in one tap.

Why: mobile isn't a smaller dashboard — it's a different question.

The map is the workspace

Customer, accounts, wires and hits form a living network. Click any node for its story and actions — the case is spatial, not tabular.

Why: money laundering is a network; the UI should look like one.

Time is the master control

A docked timeline is two-way linked to everything. Press play and watch the case unfold — funds in, layering, wires out, the alert.

Why: every investigation is a story; stories are told in time.

Everything floats, nothing hides

All 7 sections are light floating cards over the map — movable, expandable to work mode. Drag evidence out to pin it; amber threads tie pins to entities.

Why: opening a document should never mean losing the case.

Continuity is a feature

Taking over a colleague's case opens a handover brief: done vs. still open, unread notes, and "resume where they left off". ✓/⚠ dots mark every section.

Why: the most expensive moment in a case is the analyst switch.

AI in context, and accountable

The advisor knows what you're looking at. Agent runs and LLM spend are glanceable widgets — the AI's work is visible, timed and priced.

Why: trust in agents comes from seeing them work, not a black box.

Same content, new stage

This is a UX change, not a scope change. Every screen carries the content captured from the current product — the CASE-AML-0001-3 alert review (Marisol Rivera), its worksheet, evidence, datasets, audit trail and real agent economics.
